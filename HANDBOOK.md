## 📜 Section A: The Founding Charter

### 🏛️ The Founding Charter of the AI Club — Nalanda University

> *"Knowledge is the true wealth that grows when shared; true inquiry has no boundary between disciplines."*  
> — Inspired by the Nalanda Tradition (राजगृह / नालन्दा)

---

## Article I: Name and Identity
1. The collective shall be known as the **AI Club, Nalanda University** (hereinafter referred to as the "Club").
2. The Club is established under the aegis of the **MSc Data Science & AI Programme** at Nalanda University (Rajgir, Bihar, India), serving the founding Cohort 2026–28 and all subsequent graduating cohorts.
3. The official digital identity, documentation, and registry are anchored at `https://aiclub.nalandalibrary.com` and GitHub organisation `nalanda-ai-club`.
4. **Domain Ownership & Non-Transferability**: The root domain `nalandalibrary.com` (and all associated top-level infrastructure) is the exclusive, non-transferable private property of the Founder. The Club operates its digital presence on the delegated subdomain (`aiclub.nalandalibrary.com`). Under no circumstances is root domain ownership or registrar transfer permitted as part of cohort succession or governance transitions.

---

## Article II: Foundational Premise — The Multidisciplinary Imperative
1. **The Mixed-Background Reality**: Unlike conventional engineering-only collectives, the AI Club at Nalanda University brings together scholars from international relations, economics, history, humanities, ecological studies, commerce, computer science, and the physical sciences.
2. **Parity of Esteem**: Technical implementation and domain-specific inquiry are held in absolute equal regard. A sophisticated neural architecture addressing a trivial or ungrounded question is considered an incomplete inquiry; a piercing domain question without computational rigor is an incomplete inquiry. The strength of the Club lies in bridging the two.
3. **The "2-in-a-Pod" Matching Law**: All club incubation projects, case competition squads, and policy briefs must actively pair computational practitioners with domain specialists.

---

## Article III: Core Principles of Governance
1. **Default-to-Approve**: In order to maximize student velocity and drive relentless innovation, initiatives, experiments, and open-source explorations operate under a presumption of approval. Bureaucratic gatekeeping, multi-signature veto chokepoints, and artificial friction are explicitly prohibited.
2. **72-Hour Asynchronous Consent (RFC)**: Routine operational decisions, guidelines, and project approvals operate under the 72-hour silent consent model.
3. **Transparent & Open Ledger**: All finances, computing quotas, sponsorships, and organizational decisions are published openly to all members. Zero private club accounts or hidden deliberations.
4. **Perpetual Succession & The Relay Principle**: Student leadership is transient; institutional memory must be permanent. Outgoing cohorts owe incoming cohorts a mandatory 60-day structured handover, credential transfer, and advisory stewardship.
5. **Team Structure Stability & The 80% Consent Rule**: The organizational topology, executive roles, and task ownership matrix are defined in `TEAM_STRUCTURE.md` and `SOP-014`. Any proposal to alter the governance hierarchy, remove or add executive stewards, or reorganize core pod mandates requires an **affirmative supermajority consent of eighty percent (80%)** of the active cohort.

---

## Article IV: Intellectual Property & Public Good
1. **Creator Ownership**: All algorithms, models, publications, and startup intellectual property created by members remain 100% the property of their student creators. The Club claims zero equity or IP assignment.
2. **Open-by-Default Contribution**: Codebases developed under shared Club compute or community sprints shall be open-sourced under the **MIT** or **Apache 2.0** license. Curricula, reading notes, and research whitepapers shall be licensed under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC-BY-4.0)**.
3. **Attribution & Academic Integrity**: All publications and projects incubated within the Club shall acknowledge *AI Club, Nalanda University* and strictly uphold international norms of plagiarism prevention, data authenticity, and citation rigor.

---

## Article V: Responsible AI & Civilizational Ethics
1. **Ethics Grounded in Purpose**: Members shall not design, train, or deploy systems intended for deceptive manipulation, weaponization, unauthorized surveillance, or academic fraud.
2. **Privacy & Sovereign Compliance**: All data collection, processing, and curation must comply with India's **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023** and ethical human-subject research standards.
3. **Critical Assessment**: Every deployed model or published study must include a standardized **Model Card** documenting training data provenance, evaluation metrics, limitations, and potential systemic biases.

---

## Article VI: Amendments
1. **Operational Amendments**: Standard procedural updates to individual SOPs may be enacted via a 72-hour RFC (SOP-012).
2. **Structural & Constitutional Amendments**: Amendments to this Charter, core governance rights, or the foundational Team Structure (`TEAM_STRUCTURE.md`) strictly require:
   - A mandatory 7-day campus comment period.
   - An affirmative supermajority vote of at least **80% of active cohort members**.
   - Review and endorsement by the Faculty Advisory Board.

*Ratified by the Founding Cohort (2026–28) at Nalanda University, Rajgir.*

## 4. Provisions for Changing Team Structure (The 80% Supermajority Rule)

To prevent arbitrary power consolidation, hasty restructuring by small factions, or political instability across cohorts, **any structural modification to the team hierarchy is strictly protected by an 80% supermajority threshold**.

### 4.1 Scope of Structural Changes
The 80% Supermajority Rule applies to:
1. Creating, merging, renaming, or abolishing any Executive Council seat (Convenor, Tech Steward, Domain Steward, Operations Steward, Financial Steward, AI Safety Steward).
2. Modifying the mandatory RACI task ownership assignments or removing accountability layers.
3. Modifying the 60-day cohort succession framework or voting eligibility criteria.
4. Altering this amendment threshold itself.

*(Routine operational changes—such as scheduling clinics, assigning ad-hoc project pods, or updating tool versions—do NOT require 80% and proceed via standard 72h RFC consent under SOP-012).*

### 4.2 Step-by-Step Structural Amendment Procedure

```
  Step 1: Structural RFC Draft (14-Day Notice)
  - Proposer drafts an RFC under `rfcs/structural/` outlining proposed changes & rationale.
  - Requires co-sponsorship by at least 3 active cohort members from different undergrad disciplines.
       │
       ▼
  Step 2: Campus Deliberation & Townhall
  - A formal 45-minute open-floor cohort townhall is convened on the Nalanda campus.
  - Arguments for and against the restructuring are recorded openly.
       │
       ▼
  Step 3: Supermajority Secret Ballot (48-Hour Window)
  - Administered digitally to all verified registered active cohort members.
  - Criterion: At least 80% of ALL active registered members must vote AFFIRMATIVE.
  - Example: If the active cohort has 40 registered members, at least 32 members must vote YES.
       │
       ▼
  Step 4: Faculty Endorsement & Ratification
  - The Faculty Advisory Board reviews the ballot verification to ensure institutional compliance.
  - Upon sign-off, `TEAM_STRUCTURE.md` and `SOP-014` are updated and committed to `main`.
```

### 4.3 Voter Eligibility
* Only enrolled students in the active MSc Data Science & AI cohorts who are registered in the official directory (`directory.html`) and have attended at least two club activities in the preceding 60 days are eligible voting members.

## 1. Purpose
To define a resilient, non-hierarchical governance model that minimizes bureaucratic overhead, distributes decision-making power, and empowers student pods to act autonomously while maintaining accountability.

## 2. Guiding Principles
* **Servant Leadership**: Club leadership exists to clear obstacles, secure resources, and unblock member initiatives—not to police or micro-manage.
* **Dual-Stewardship**: Core functions pair a senior-year steward with a junior-year co-lead to build unbroken institutional continuity.
* **Zero Artificial Hierarchy**: Project leaders lead within their project pods without needing executive titles.

## 4. Role Specifications

### 4.1 Club Convenor (Elected / Consensual)
* **Term**: 1 Academic Year (September to August).
* **Responsibilities**:
  1. Act as primary liaison to Nalanda University administration, School Dean, and visiting dignitaries.
  2. Protect the "Default-to-Approve" culture; intervene when friction or bottlenecks arise.
  3. Lead quarterly transparency reports and annual cohort transitions.
  4. Ensure all members in the directory have access to shared tools and compute channels.

### 4.2 Technical Steward(s) (1 to 2 members)
* **Responsibilities**:
  1. Administer the GitHub organization (`nalanda-ai-club`), cloud accounts, and shared GPU/API quotas.
  2. Maintain automated CI/CD tools, the documentation package, and the public directory website (`Data-science-club`).
  3. Provide architectural feedback on incubated technical projects.
  4. Ensure zero credential leaks; perform quarterly key rotations.

### 4.3 Domain Stewards (2 to 3 members across non-CS fields)
* **Fields**: International Relations, Economics, Historical Studies, Ecology/Agriculture, Philosophy/Ethics.
* **Responsibilities**:
  1. Lead the articulation of real-world research questions and policy challenges.
  2. Guide data collection, qualitative fieldwork methods, and domain relevance.
  3. Ensure non-engineering students are never marginalized and actively co-lead technical initiatives.
  4. Curate interdisciplinary papers for the weekly Reading Group.

### 4.4 Operations & Community Steward (1 to 2 members)
* **Responsibilities**:
  1. Manage event scheduling, venue bookings on campus, and guest hospitality.
  2. Moderate official communications (WhatsApp/Telegram/Slack/Email announcements).
  3. Ensure meeting notes, post-mortems, and session recordings are cataloged within 48 hours.
  4. Track peer mock interviews and resume clinics during placement seasons.

---

## 5. Pod Autonomy & The Rule of 2
1. Any two active members (at least one computational + at least one domain specialist) may self-constitute an official **Project Pod**.
2. Pods do not require prior committee approval to begin research, hold meetings, or prototype code.
3. Pods automatically unlock **Tier 1 Compute** (Google Colab / Hugging Face Spaces) immediately upon submitting a one-page project brief.

---

## 6. Accountability & Term Limits
* No member may hold the Convenorship for more than one full year.
* If any steward fails to fulfill duties for 30 consecutive days, an open RFC for co-lead reassignment may be initiated by any 3 active members.

---

## 7. Master Team Structure & Structural Amendment Constraint
* The definitive task ownership mapping and RACI assignments are maintained in `TEAM_STRUCTURE.md` and `SOP-014`.
* Any proposal to modify the core team structure, add/remove executive steward seats, or alter pod mandates requires an **affirmative supermajority consent of eighty percent (80%)** of the active cohort.

# SOP-012: RFC and Consent-Based Decision-Making

| Metadata | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| **SOP Code** | SOP-012 |
| **Category** | 01 - Governance & Succession |
| **Effective Date** | 2026-09-01 |
| **Target Audience** | All Club Members |
| **Principle** | "Silence implies consent; objections require alternatives." |

---

## 1. Purpose
To eliminate lengthy, circular committee debates by establishing an asynchronous, written, transparent **Request for Comments (RFC)** mechanism modeled after open-source engineering governance (IETF / Python PEP / Rust RFC).

---

## 2. When is an RFC Required?

| Action / Initiative | RFC Required? | Decision Path |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Starting a new project prototype / research paper | **NO** | Autonomous (Rule of 2, SOP-030) |
| Requesting Tier 1 Compute (< ₹1,000 / Colab) | **NO** | Instant self-registration (SOP-031) |
| Requesting Tier 2 Compute (₹1,000 - ₹10,000) | **NO** | Technical Steward 24h review |
| Spending Club funds > ₹10,000 | **YES** | Standard 72h RFC |
| Amending operational SOPs | **YES** | Standard 72h RFC |
| Modifying Team Structure / Governance seats | **YES** | **Structural RFC (80% Supermajority Cohort Consent)** |
| Signing partnerships with external companies / sponsors | **YES** | Standard 72h RFC + Faculty Review |
| Hosting a major multi-college hackathon / conference | **YES** | Standard 72h RFC |

---

## 3. The 72-Hour Consent Decision Cycle

```
  T = 0                     T + 48h                   T + 72h
  (RFC Published)           (Review & Feedback)       (Consensus Reached)
     │                         │                         │
     ▼                         ▼                         ▼
  PR opened on              Members review;           Zero unresolved blocking
  `ops/rfcs/` with          suggest refinements       objections -> Merged &
  template metadata         or constructive edits     Auto-Approved
```

### Step 1: Draft and Scaffold
1. The proposer runs the CLI scaffolder:
   ```bash
   ai-ops scaffold --type rfc --title "Introduce-Dedicated-RunPod-GPU-Cluster"
   ```
2. Fill out the sections in `templates/rfc-template.md`:
   - Motivation & Context
   - Proposed Specification
   - Budget / Resource Implications
   - Trade-offs and Rejected Alternatives
   - Multidisciplinary Impact

### Step 2: Publish & Announce
1. Open a Pull Request in the Club operations repository (`DayDreamer-commits/ai-club-ops`).
2. Post a direct link to the PR in the official communication channel with the tag `[RFC-VOTE]`.
3. The 72-hour countdown begins immediately upon posting.

### Step 3: Consent vs. Objection Norms
* **Consent is the Default**: A proposal does not need 100% "yes" votes. If members do not object, they are presumed to consent.
* **The Rule of Constructive Objection**: An objection is only valid if:
  1. It identifies a demonstrable harm or risk to the Club, members, or Nalanda University.
  2. The objector provides a concrete, workable alternative.
  3. Personal taste, bikeshedding, or passive skepticism are not valid blocking objections.
* **Friendly Amendments**: The author may amend the PR during the 72-hour window. If major amendments occur, an extension of 24 hours is automatically granted.

### Step 4: Resolution & Merging
* If no blocking objection remains at the 72-hour mark: The RFC is marked **APPROVED** and merged into `rfcs/approved/`.
* If an impasse arises: The Convenor hosts a 20-minute time-boxed synchronous discussion. If no consensus emerges, a simple 60% majority vote of active members decides.

## 1. Purpose
To maintain unambiguous operational task ownership, establish clear Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs) for every club standard operating procedure, and protect the foundational team structure against hasty or unilateral political disruptions through an 80% supermajority amendment threshold.

---

## 2. The Directly Responsible Individual (DRI) Model
1. **Single Point of Ownership**: For every recurring operational duty, exactly one member is designated as the DRI (with one identified backup DRI).
2. **Accountability Without Silos**: While the DRI is responsible for task completion, decisions remain collaborative and transparent.
3. **Task Delegation**: A DRI may recruit volunteers or project pod contributors to execute sub-tasks, but retains ultimate ownership for the deliverable and timeline.

---

## 3. Task Ownership Registry
All recurring tasks and their associated SOPs are indexed in the Master RACI Matrix in `TEAM_STRUCTURE.md`:
* **Governance Sync**: Convenor (SOP-010, SOP-011)
* **RFC Processing**: Operations Steward (SOP-012)
* **Directory & Onboarding**: Web Platform DRI (SOP-020)
* **Cross-Disciplinary Pod Pairing**: Domain Steward (SOP-021)
* **Code of Conduct Mediation**: Domain Steward & Convenor (SOP-022)
* **Cloud Quotas & Key Vault**: Cloud & Compute DRI (SOP-031)
* **Open Source & IP Hygiene**: Open Source DRI (SOP-032)
* **Hackathon War-Rooms**: Competition Captain (SOP-033)
* **Literature Dissection**: Literature Lead (SOP-040)
* **Guest Colloquiums**: Guest Lead (SOP-041)
* **Reimbursements & Ledger**: Financial Steward (SOP-050, SOP-052)
* **Model Cards & Privacy Audits**: AI Safety Steward (SOP-060, SOP-061)

---

## 4. The 80% Supermajority Structural Amendment Protocol

### 4.1 Constitutional Principle
The core governance topology, executive council seats, and stewardship roles defined in `TEAM_STRUCTURE.md` represent the hard consensus of the founding cohort. While the Club embraces relentless experimentation at the project level, the organizational structure must remain stable and immune to factionalism.

### 4.2 Protected Elements
The following modifications strictly require **80% Supermajority Cohort Consent**:
* Altering the number, titles, or authority of Executive Council Stewards.
* Removing, dissolving, or structurally reorganizing specialized operational pods.
* Altering the DRI task ownership matrix or abolishing core SOP responsibilities.
* Changing the voting franchise or reducing the 80% threshold itself.

### 4.3 Voting Procedure & Threshold Calculations
1. **Notice**: The Structural RFC must be published with at least 14 calendar days of advance campus notice.
2. **Townhall**: At least one open hybrid townhall must be conducted before voting opens.
3. **Voting Window**: Electronic ballot open for exactly 48 hours.
4. **Passage Criterion**:
   $$\text{Affirmative Votes} \ge 0.80 \times \text{Total Registered Active Cohort Members}$$
   *(Abstentions and non-votes count as non-affirmative to prevent small vocal groups from passing structural changes).*
5. **Ratification**: The Faculty Advisory Board validates the election tally and ratifies the change.

* Format:
  - 3 minutes: Domain specialists present 1 unresolved question or data challenge (e.g. "Mapping ancient trade routes from Pali/Sanskrit palm-leaf manuscripts" or "Predicting groundwater depletion in South Bihar").
  - 3 minutes: Technical students pitch algorithmic techniques (e.g. "Vision transformers for OCR on degraded scripts" or "Spatiotemporal graph neural networks").

## 5. Pod Operational Cadence
* **Sprint Length**: 2-week agile iterations.

## 1. Purpose
To cultivate intellectual depth by running structured, dual-lens reading groups that analyze both cutting-edge algorithmic mechanisms and their socio-political, economic, and ethical ramifications.

---

## 2. The Dual-Lens Reading Model
Every paper selected is unpacked through two complementary vantage points:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   SELECTED PAPER                       │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            │
              ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
              ▼                           ▼
   Lens A: Technical & Math    Lens B: Domain & Policy
   - Architectural novelty     - Economic incentives
   - Loss functions & bounds   - Institutional impact
   - Compute complexity        - Historical precedent
   - Benchmark validity        - Regulatory/safety risks
```

---

## 3. Weekly Operational Routine

### Monday (Paper Selection & Rotation)
* The designated Facilitator of the week posts 2 candidate papers in the `#reading-group` channel.

Every model published to Hugging Face, GitHub, or academic conferences must include a standardized `MODEL_CARD.md` based on `templates/model-card-template.md`.

### Required Sections:
1. **Model Details**: Architecture, parameter count, base checkpoint, training hardware.

* **Q1 (Dual Use)**: Could this model be trivially adapted to generate biological/chemical threats, automated cyberattacks, or hate speech?

* **Q3 (Hallucination Harm)**: If deployed in high-stakes domains (healthcare, legal, public welfare), what are the consequences of false positive/negative predictions?
* **Q4 (Algorithmic Fairness)**: Does the model systematically underperform on marginalized linguistic, regional, or socio-economic demographics?

If any risk is identified, the pod must document mitigations (filtering, guardrails, confidence thresholds) directly in the Model Card.
